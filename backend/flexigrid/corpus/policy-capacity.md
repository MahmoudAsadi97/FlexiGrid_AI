## Why household peaks matter in Flanders

Since 2023, the distribution-grid component of a Flemish electricity bill depends partly on the household's monthly peak demand rather than only on energy volume. The distribution system operator measures average power per quarter-hour; the highest quarter-hour of the month sets the monthly peak, and the billed value is the average of the monthly peaks over the last twelve months, with a floor of 2.5 kW. Digital-meter households therefore save money by keeping simultaneous consumption flat, not just by consuming less.

## The FlexiGrid connection limit

This household's controllable load is capped at 4.6 kW. The cap keeps the total household demand, including the roughly 1 kW of uncontrolled base load, safely below the level that would set a new monthly quarter-hour peak. The planner must verify the cap for every hour of a proposed schedule; a single violating hour can raise the capacity component of the bill for twelve months.

## Interaction with scheduling

Spreading flexible appliances across hours is usually cheaper under the capacity tariff than stacking them into the single cheapest hour. When an hourly energy price and the capacity cap conflict, the cap always wins: it is a hard constraint, while the price is an optimization preference.
